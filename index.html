Method & provenance

What this app knows—
and what it cannot.

Traditional basis

This app uses the conventional North Indian Ashtakoota sequence: Varna (1), Vashya (2), Tara (3), Yoni (4), Graha Maitri (5), Gana (6), Bhakoot (7), and Nadi (8). A 36-point table is documented in modern Jyotisha teaching works such as K. S. Charak’s Elements of Vedic Astrology. B. V. Raman’s Muhurtha, chapter XI, gives a widely used marriage-compatibility treatment.

The Brihat Parāśara Horā Śāstra is linked as important wider horoscopy and nakshatra context in the Parāśara tradition. We do not claim that its oldest recoverable layer supplies this exact modern 36-point implementation.

Computational assumptions
  • Geocentric Moon longitude uses a compact, low-precision orbital model with major perturbations—not a professional ephemeris.
  • A linear Chitrapaksha/Lahiri ayanamsha approximation is used. Near a nakshatra or pada boundary, verify with a current panchanga or Swiss Ephemeris.
  • Birthplace is not geocoded; location affects this version only through the UTC offset you enter.
  • Yoni and Vashya use disclosed compact relationship tables. Regional tables differ.
  • No Nadi/Bhakoot cancellation rules, Mangala analysis, Navamsha, Dashas, house comparison, or astrologer judgment are applied.
  • Nakshatra recommendations test the midpoint of each pada and keep that star’s strongest pada; they do not represent a complete second birth chart.
